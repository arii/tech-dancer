## Abstract

We analyzed 847 placements across 12 national WCS events to determine whether scoring variance in the "Connection" criteria exceeds that of "Timing" in the Intermediate division. We identified a 14% higher variance in connection scores, suggesting lower judge consensus on this metric.

## Background & Motivation

Why does this question matter to a working dancer? Every dancer has had the experience of getting a "1" from one judge and a "7" from another. Understanding where that variance comes from helps in focusing your training.

## Methodology

**Data Source:** Publicly available WCS competition results from 2024-2025.

**Sample Size:** 847 placements across 12 events.

**Tools Used:** Python / pandas, Monte Carlo simulation.

## Results

> **Finding 1:** The standard deviation for "Connection" scores (σ = 2.3) was 14% higher than for "Timing" scores (σ = 2.0).

## Discussion

What do the results mean for a dancer? Timing is a objective binary (on or off). Connection is a subjective spectrum. To win, you must be irrefutable on timing and "loud" enough on connection for the judge to see it from the sideline.

## Conclusion & Takeaway

Master the irrefutables (timing, footwork) to anchor your score, then use "loud" mechanics to decrease connection variance.
