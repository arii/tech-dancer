Here is the uncomfortable math: a typical event ballroom runs its sound system at 95 to 105 decibels, and the safe exposure limit at 100 dB is about fifteen minutes. You danced four hours. Nobody talks about hearing at dance events because hearing loss is invisible, cumulative, and — by the time you notice — permanent. This is the gear post I wish someone had written ten years ago.

## Why Not Just Any Foam Earplugs

Foam plugs muffle everything equally, which kills the frequencies you actually use while dancing: the attack of the lead, the top end of the melody, your partner's timing. Foam plugs make music feel like it is happening underwater, so people wear them once and quit. Filtered (high-fidelity) earplugs attenuate volume evenly across frequencies. The music gets quieter; it does not get worse.

## The Part Nobody Believes Until They Try It

You will hear your partner *better* with filtered earplugs. Loud rooms cause sensory overload, and lowering the noise floor makes the subtle connection cues easier to read, not harder. Several of my favorite social dances of the past year happened with plugs in.

## Making the Habit Stick

- Keep the case clipped to your dance bag keyring — protection you cannot find does not get worn.
- Wear them at the DJ booth-adjacent tables even when not dancing; that is where the peak exposure is.
- If you leave an event and the world sounds muffled or you hear ringing, that is a temporary threshold shift. Repeated often enough, it stops being temporary.

Your ears are a 40-year dance career's worth of infrastructure. Two pairs of filtered earplugs cost less than one event pass.
