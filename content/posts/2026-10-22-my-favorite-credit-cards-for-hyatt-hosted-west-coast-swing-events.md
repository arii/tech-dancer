## The short version

This post is about one cost: the hotel at Hyatt-hosted West Coast Swing events. For everything else — transit, competitions, event registration, workshops — I use a different favorite, the Wells Fargo Attune, which has its own post in this series.

The event pass is rarely the expensive part of a dance weekend. The three or four hotel nights are — and you want to be in the event hotel, not driving across town at 3 a.m. after social dancing. Two cards cover the Hyatt nights:

- **World of Hyatt Credit Card ($95/year).** An anniversary free night at a Category 1–4 Hyatt, every year. Used at an event hotel, that night is usually worth more than the fee.
- **Chase Sapphire Reserve ($795/year).** The expensive one. I keep it mainly because its points still transfer 1:1 to World of Hyatt — since October 1, 2026, Chase's cheaper cards no longer do.

*The World of Hyatt Credit Card and Chase Sapphire Reserve.*

- **Annual fee:** $95.
- **Anniversary free night:** one free night a year at any Category 1–4 Hyatt.
- **A second free night** after spending $15,000 on the card in a calendar year.
- **Status:** automatic Discoverist status while the card is open, plus 5 tier-qualifying night credits a year — and 2 more per $5,000 spent.
- **Earning at Hyatt:** up to 9 points per $1 — 4 bonus points from the card on top of member base points — plus bonus categories like dining and transit.

**How I use it:** Hyatt stays go on this card, and I time the anniversary night to an event weekend. If that night goes unused, the argument collapses — an expiring certificate is a $95 donation, not a strategy.

## Card 2: Chase Sapphire Reserve — now the only 1:1 door into Hyatt

The Reserve's fee rose to $795 in the June 2025 refresh. I wouldn't keep it for lounges or credits alone. I keep it for Hyatt — and for what changed this fall.

**On October 1, 2026, Chase cut the Hyatt transfer rate for the Sapphire Preferred and Ink cards from 1:1 to 4:3.** Transfer 100,000 points from a Preferred now and you get 75,000 Hyatt points. The Reserve kept 1:1: 1,000 Chase points still become 1,000 Hyatt points. That makes it the only mainstream Chase card that moves points into Hyatt at full value — and Hyatt points go far, because award nights still price off a published category chart instead of purely dynamic pricing.

The rest of the card still has to earn its keep:

- **$300 annual travel credit**, applied automatically to a broad range of travel purchases — no portal booking, no activation. Event travel triggers it on its own. This is the easiest $300 of the fee to recover.
- **Earning:** 8 points per $1 on travel booked through Chase Travel, 4 per $1 on flights and hotels booked directly, 3 per $1 on dining — the categories a dance weekend actually runs through.
- **Flexibility:** the same points transfer to airline partners, so I keep them as Chase points until an award night is available, then transfer. Once transferred, they're Hyatt points for good.
- **Lounges and basics:** Sapphire Lounges plus Priority Pass, a credit of up to $120 every four years for Global Entry, TSA PreCheck, or NEXUS, and no foreign transaction fees.
- **Trip protections:** trip cancellation/interruption, trip delay, and baggage coverage, plus purchase protection against damage or theft.
- **Rental car coverage:** primary theft and collision coverage when you charge the rental and decline the counter's coverage.
- I ignore The Edit and lifestyle credits because they don't fit my actual dance travel spending.

**[Earn 100,000 bonus points with Sapphire Reserve® or 75,000 bonus points with Sapphire Preferred® — apply here](https://www.referyourchasecard.com/19y/K7GDL8QMKO)** — I can be rewarded if you apply here and are approved for either card.

**The honest test:** add up the Hyatt points you actually transferred and redeemed last year, plus the credits you used without trying. If that clearly beats $795, the Reserve earns its slot. If your Hyatt redemptions are small, the Preferred at $95 — even at 4:3 — or the Hyatt card alone may be smarter.

## How I use the two cards together

- **Book on points when cash rates spike.** Event weekends compress demand into one property, so cash rates jump — exactly when a category-based award chart favors you. Transfer from the Reserve at 1:1 and book with points.
- **Anchor with the free night.** Use the Hyatt card's anniversary certificate for one night and points for the rest; a four-night weekend can drop to a fraction of its cash price.
- **Pay cash costs with the right card.** Paid Hyatt stays go on the Hyatt card for its bonus earning; flights, dining, and other travel go on the Reserve, earning the points that become next event's Hyatt points.

## The caveats

- **Events move.** The Hyatt advantage lasts only while your events stay in Hyatts. Verify the venue each year; don't stockpile points for a hotel an event no longer uses.
- **Award space isn't guaranteed.** Certificates cover Category 1–4 only, and event blocks sell out. Book early.
- **Interest erases everything.** Every number here assumes you pay both cards in full, every month.
- **Terms change.** Fees, transfer rates, and credits have all changed in the last eighteen months. Check Chase's and Hyatt's current terms before applying or renewing; figures here reflect October 2026.
