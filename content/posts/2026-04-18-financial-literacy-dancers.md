Welcome back to the Tech-Dancer blog! Usually, we spend our time analyzing the biomechanics of a perfect West Coast Swing whip or finding the optimal travel tech for long convention weekends. But today, we are applying that same engineering rigor to a different kind of system: your finances.

Traveling for dance, paying for private lessons, and keeping up with the latest gear adds up quickly. If you want to sustain this lifestyle long-term, you need a solid financial architecture.

*Disclaimer: I am a roboticist and a dancer, not a licensed financial advisor, CPA, or fiduciary. The following is simply the financial framework I use to optimize my own life. Always consult a certified professional before making major money moves!*

Here is my blueprint for automating wealth and balancing the cost of the dance circuit:

## 1. Max Out Your 401(k)

Think of your 401(k) as the backend infrastructure of your financial algorithm. It should run quietly in the background without you ever having to look at it. For 2026, the IRS contribution limit for employee deferrals is $24,500. If your budget allows, automate your payroll deductions to hit this max. Not only does this lower your taxable income right now, but it also allows your money to compound tax-deferred over decades. Take advantage of the system.

## 2. The Golden Rule: Treat Credit Cards Like Debit Cards

If you look at my wallet, you’ll see some premium, high-annual-fee credit cards. I happily pay those hefty annual fees, but only because I ruthlessly take advantage of their perks—like lounge access on my flights to out-of-state dance conventions, free checked bags, and Hyatt hotel upgrades.

However, there is a hard rule to this strategy: You must treat your credit cards exactly like debit cards. If the cash isn't sitting in my checking account at this exact second, I do not swipe the card. Carrying a balance and paying 20%+ in interest will instantly obliterate any value you get from travel points. Pay your statement balance in full, every single month. Zero exceptions.

## 3. Under the Income Limit? Get a Roth IRA!

If your 401(k) is your backend server, your Roth IRA is your high-performance cache. You fund it with after-tax money, meaning you've already paid the IRS. The beauty is that the money grows tax-free, and you can withdraw it tax-free in retirement.

If you haven't already opened one, do it today. For 2026, the maximum contribution limit is $7,500. However, there are income limits: if you are a single filer and your Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) is under $153,000 for the 2026 tax year, you can make the full contribution. Max this out if you can!

## 4. Tackling Event FOMO Head-On

Let’s talk about the psychological side of the ledger. In the West Coast Swing scene (and the tech world), the Fear Of Missing Out (FOMO) is intense. Every weekend, your Instagram feed is flooded with friends flying to a new convention, wearing the latest dance sneakers, and going out for 3 AM post-dance boba.

You have to tackle FOMO head-on and engineer your boundaries. You do not have to attend every single event to level up your dancing. It is mathematically impossible to do it all without burning out your body and your bank account.

Instead, look at the calendar and choose your priority events for the year. Budget for them, book them early, and let the rest go without an ounce of guilt. Local socials and mindful practice sessions often yield better technical growth than a sleep-deprived weekend away anyway.

Optimize your spending, build your financial pipeline, and I’ll see you on the dance floor!
