In robotics, we never run an experiment without a telemetry plan. We need to know exactly what the system is doing, where it’s failing, and how to replicate the success.

I’ve found that my West Coast Swing private lessons are the exact same. At $100–$200+ an hour, you aren't just paying for dancing; you’re paying for a data transfer. Time is your most precious commodity—don't waste it. To make that "data" stick, you need a system.

## 1. External Telemetry: The "Dance Dummy" & Video

If you want objective data, you need an observer. If your coach is okay with it, bring a partner to act as your "human data logger."

*   **The Scribe:** Have them take notes or video specific segments during the lesson so you can stay physically present in the movement.
*   **The Final Recap:** Always record a summary at the end. I review these videos with my instructors later to standardize my performance and identify exactly why I’m getting marked up or down.
*   **The Truth of Video:** Video doesn't lie about posture or timing. It’s the closest thing we have to a system log for our bodies.

## 2. The "n=1 to 3" Rule: Avoid System Overload

It is a mistake to try to "fix everything" in 60 minutes. That’s how you instill bad habits or end up frustrated.

*   **Distill the Data:** Whenever I finish a lesson or workshop, I try to leave with at most three specific things (and often just one).
*   **Protect Your Headspace:** Be realistic. If I notice I’m not in the right headspace to learn, I leave. It is better to walk away than to spend money "hard-coding" mistakes because you're tired or unfocused.
*   **Intentional Reps:** Don't just do a variation 100x because a formula said so. Use tools like scatting to confirm you’re actually on time, or mirrors for real-time visual assessment. Arbitrary reps lead to boredom; intentional practice leads to progress.

## 3. Error Handling & Recovery: Don't Follow Off a Bridge

A private lesson is where you build your "fail-safes." WCS is a partnership, and sometimes the system encounters an unexpected input.

*   **Navigating the Destination:** I work with my instructors on how to provide ideas for the partnership. If a leader doesn't catch a suggestion, I don't "follow them off a bridge"—I use level changes or delayed doubles to stay on beat and anchor until we recover.
*   **The Post-Processing:** After the intensity of a lesson or a long day of competing with incredible dancers, manage your resources. Go to sleep, dance, or get in the hot tub. You put yourself out there; don't waste energy feeling like a loser because of a subjective result.
