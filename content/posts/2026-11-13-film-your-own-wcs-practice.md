Every dancer eventually hears "just film yourself" — and most stop after one cringe-filled viewing. The problem is not the camera; it is that nobody explains what to do with the footage. Filmed practice is the single highest-leverage training habit I know, and it works in a living room. If you have not set up a space yet, start with our [home practice space guide](/blog/2026-06-20-ultimate-wcs-home-practice-space).

## The Setup: One Tripod, Two Angles

You need exactly two camera positions for WCS, and you alternate between sessions:

- **On the slot (end-on).** The camera at one end of your dance slot. This is the angle that shows your slot discipline — whether your anchor travels, whether you drift out of the lane. It is the angle that hurts the most and fixes the most.
- **Broadside.** Camera on the side, perpendicular to the slot. This shows posture, frame, and footwork detail.

A basic phone tripod is all this requires. Put the phone at chest height, roughly the height of a partner's connection.

## Music You Can Actually Anchor To

Practicing in silence produces patterns; practicing to music produces timing. A small portable speaker on the floor next to the tripod keeps the beat where your feet are — and phone speakers bury the percussion your anchor needs to sit inside.

<notice type="affiliate" id="portable-speaker"></notice>

## The Review Method: One Question Per Take

Do not watch footage like a movie. Watch it like a scout, one question at a time:

1. **Take one:** Does my anchor stop where it should, or does it drift toward the lead?
2. **Take two:** Am I on time, or am I early on every "&"?
3. **Take three:** Where is my weight on the last beat of every pattern?

Three questions per session, and stop. Ten minutes of focused review beats an hour of watching yourself dance beautifully to music you ignored.

## Mirrors Are the Cheaper Half of the Feedback Loop

The camera is for full patterns; a mirror is for micro-adjustments in real time. Stick-on mirrors at chest height on a closet door give you instant frame and posture feedback during drills — no phone, no re-take.

## Keep One Clip a Month

The progress you cannot feel is the progress a video archive proves. Save one unedited take a month; next year, the before/after is the most motivating artifact in your dancing.
