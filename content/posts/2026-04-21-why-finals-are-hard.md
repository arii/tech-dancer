It’s one of the most frustrating experiences in West Coast Swing: you’re dancing better than ever, you feel great about your heats, and yet... no recall.

If you are an "above-average" dancer—someone consistently in the top 30-40% of their division—making finals is often less about your skill and more about the statistical nature of the competition.

### The Cutoff Band

Imagine a division with 40 dancers. If 12 are called to finals, there is usually a clear "top 5" that almost every judge agrees on. However, for spots 6 through 15, the "cutoff band" is incredibly thin. The difference between 12th place (finalist) and 13th place (no-recall) might be a single judge's diagonal mark.

### Why One Result is Noisy

Judging is subjective and fast. A judge might miss your best triple because they were looking at another couple, or you might have been caught in a "traffic jam" on the floor during your strongest song.

Because the "no-recall" happens so close to the cutoff, a single event's result is "noisy"—it doesn't necessarily reflect a drop in your skill.

### What to Track Instead

Instead of focusing on "Final vs. No-Final," look at:
- **Judge Consistency:** Did you get marks from 1 judge or 4?
- **Internal Feeling:** Did you execute the techniques you’ve been practicing?
- **Video Review:** Does your dancing look the way you want it to look?

### What to do after a no-recall

1. **Wait 24 Hours:** Don't check the scores immediately while emotions are high.
2. **Review with a Mentor:** Show your video to someone you trust for objective feedback.
3. **Look at the Data:** Check out our [Competition Metrics](/posts/competition-metrics) post to understand the broader patterns of the event.

Remember, the goal is long-term improvement, not short-term validation.
