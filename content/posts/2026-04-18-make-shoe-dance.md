## Suede Your Dance Shoes

Dedicated dance shoes can be expensive and often lack the comfort of your favorite everyday sneakers. My preferred approach is to "upgrade" high-comfort sneakers or flats using adhesive suede. This $15 DIY hack allows you to turn almost any pair of shoes into professional-grade dance footwear.

### Step-by-Step Tutorial

Follow these steps to ensure your suede modification lasts through even the longest social sets.

1.  **Clean the sole**: Thoroughly clean the rubber sole of your shoe with isopropyl alcohol. This removes oils, dirt, and manufacturing residues that would otherwise compromise the adhesive.
2.  **Trace and Cut**: Place your shoe on the paper backing of the suede sheet. Trace the outline of the area you want to cover, then cut the suede to shape using heavy-duty scissors.
3.  **Apply suede**: Peel off the adhesive backing and carefully align the suede with the sole. Press firmly across the entire surface, starting from the center and working outwards to eliminate air bubbles.
4.  **Let it cure**: For the strongest bond, allow the adhesive to cure for at least 24 hours before dancing on them.

### Placement Strategy Comparison

The way you position the suede significantly impacts your traction and "road feel" on the dance floor.

#### 1. Ball Only
![Ball Only Coverage](/images/gear/diy/suede-ball.svg)
*   **Pros:** Maximum spinning capability; allows for "braking" power by engaging the rubber heel.
*   **Cons:** Can feel inconsistent when weight shifting; risk of the suede edge peeling if not applied perfectly.

#### 2. Split Coverage
![Split Coverage](/images/gear/diy/suede-split.svg)
*   **Pros:** Balanced traction; provides grip for both pivots and controlled stops.
*   **Cons:** More complex to cut and apply; twice as many edges that could potentially catch or peel.

#### 3. Full Coverage
![Full Coverage](/images/gear/diy/suede-full.svg)
*   **Pros:** My preferred method. Provides a uniform, consistent friction profile across the entire foot. Most predictable response on unpredictable hotel carpets or sticky social floors.
*   **Cons:** Uses more material; slightly reduces the natural flexibility of very thin-soled shoes.
